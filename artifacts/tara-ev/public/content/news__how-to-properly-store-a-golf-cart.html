How to properly store a golf cart?

  Proper storage is essential to extend the life of golf carts. Issues often arise from improper storage, causing deterioration and corrosion of internal components. Whether preparing for off-season storage, long-term parking, or just making room, understanding proper storage techniques is crucial. Here are some key steps to follow if you want to store your golf cart better:

  1. Correct Parking

  When parking, it’s best to park on a flat surface and avoid uneven ground. If the golf cart is parked on a slope, this will cause the tires to be subjected to tremendous pressure from the ground, causing them to deform. In severe cases, it can also deform the wheels. So, it’s essential to park your vehicle on a flat surface to keep the tires from getting damaged.

  2. Thorough cleaning and inspection

  Clean your golf cart thoroughly before storage. Remove dirt and debris, wash the exterior, clean interior seats, and inspect the battery, tires, and other parts for damage.Keeping your golf cart clean and well-maintained before storage will help prevent damage and make it easier to get it back up and running when needed.

  3. Battery charging

  If your golf cart is electric, the battery needs to be fully charged before storing the golf cart. This is important to avoid battery loss and potential damage during long periods of inactivity. We also recommend properly charging the battery when storing it for an extended period to maintain its effectiveness and extend its life.

  4. Choose the right storage space

  Choose a clean, dry, well-ventilated storage area that is protected from harsh weather. If possible, store your golf cart indoors and avoid exposing it to sunlight to protect it from extreme temperatures, humidity, and UV rays, which can cause damage to the paint, interior, and electrical components. Proper storage will help keep your golf cart in good condition and extend its life.

  5. Use of protective covers

  Consider the right cover designed for a golf cart to protect the vehicle from dust, moisture, and sunlight during storage. High-quality covers help prevent scratches, fading, and weather-related damage, protecting the cart’s exterior and interior.

  6. Raise wheels or adjust tires

  To prevent flat spots on your tires, consider lifting your golf cart off the ground. Ground it with a hydraulic lift or a jack stand. If it is not possible to lift the cart, moving the cart periodically or slightly deflating the tires will help prevent tire damage during long-term storage.

  7. Follow the manufacturer’s guidelines

  Refer to the manufacturer’s guide for specific storage recommendations and maintenance procedures tailored to your golf cart model. Different types and brands of golf carts may have unique storage requirements, such as specific battery maintenance, lubrication points, or additional steps to prepare the cart for storage.

  8. Stationary vehicles

  Properly store unattended golf carts to prevent theft. Use wheel locks and immobilizers for security.

  9. Regular maintenance checks

  Perform regular maintenance checks during storage, including battery and fluid level checks, to address any emerging issues.Regular maintenance checks help to identify and fix potential problems before they become more serious.

  In conclusion

  By following these steps, you’ll ensure your golf cart remains in optimal condition, ready for use when needed, and your investment is well protected.
