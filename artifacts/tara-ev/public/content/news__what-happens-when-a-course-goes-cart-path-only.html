What Happens When a Course Goes Cart Path Only?

When there is continuous rain, frost, or insufficient drainage, a course will implement Cart Path Only. Under this rule, golf carts can still be used, but must travel along designated paths and cannot enter fairways, rough, or temporarily closed areas of the green.

This rule changes how players move from teeing off to the end of a round. The course needs to manage green protection, pace of play, path traffic, and member communication simultaneously. If only a notice is posted at the front desk without adjusting the day’s operations, Cart Path Only can easily become a source of player complaints and on-course delays.

 

More Walking

 

Under normal conditions, players can park their golf carts near their tees. Under Cart Path Only, carts can only be parked on the path, and players must walk to their tees, hit their balls, and then return.

What truly slows things down is usually not a long walk, but the repeated retrieval of clubs. If a player arrives at their ball and realizes they’ve misjudged the distance, they’ll have to return to the cart to change clubs, and this single trip can affect the entire group. Therefore, the front desk, starter, and cart signage should all remind players to carry two to three clubs they might need.

The impact of extra walking is more pronounced for older players or members with mobility issues. Courses should explain the day’s Cart Policy in advance during booking confirmation or check-in, allowing players to decide whether to continue playing before teeing off, rather than only learning about the restriction on the first hole.

 

Slower Play

 

Cart Path Only usually extends the round, but delays don’t occur evenly on every hole.

When players in the same group have their balls on opposite sides of the fairway, they need to start from the same path and walk in different directions. Longer par-4 and par-5 holes tend to accumulate waiting time more easily; the impact is also more noticeable on holes where the path is farther from the fairway.

Courses can adjust the Tee Time Interval appropriately based on site conditions and have Course Marshals focus on holes prone to congestion. Instead of constantly urging players on the course, it’s better to explain the correct club handling, parking locations, and driving rules before tee time.

 

Path Traffic

 

After vehicles leave the fairway, traffic congestion concentrates on the cart path. Short queues can occur at narrow bends, slopes, near the green, and at intersections of paths shared by multiple holes.

Some players park their cars in the middle of the path to wait for their teammates, forcing vehicles behind them to wait. After rain, the edges of the path are softer, and frequent maneuvering to avoid them can damage the greens, shifting the protection focus from the fairway to the rim.

The management team can mark temporary parking areas in advance, clearly indicating which locations are suitable for waiting and which sections must remain clear. For courses that frequently implement Cart Path Only, path width, turning space, and drainage conditions should be included in long-term maintenance plans, rather than being addressed only after each rain.

 

Rules by Zone

 

A rain shower doesn’t leave all holes in the same condition. Low-lying areas may still be flooded, while slopes and well-drained holes may be able to accommodate vehicles.

Therefore, uniformly enforcing Cart Path Only across the entire course is not always the most suitable option. Courses can break down the rules into different zones: holes with severe waterlogging should maintain open paths, areas recovering quickly should use the 90-Degree Rule, and holes with stable conditions should remain open.

The problem is that these zoning rules are difficult to enforce solely with paper instructions. Players may forget which holes allow exits or accidentally enter closed areas in unmarked locations. Staff reminding each car also increases workload.

 

GPS Control

 

With a GPS Fleet Management System, courses can transform Cart Path Only from a verbal rule into an enforceable vehicle management measure.

The Tara GPS system supports Geofence Management, Zone Rules, Real-time Cart Tracking, Vehicle Speed ​​Monitoring, and In-cart Messaging. Administrators can set restrictions by hole or zone to prevent vehicles from entering waterlogged areas, repaired greens, or areas closed for the day.

When driving rules change, notifications can be sent directly to the car screens. Players see “Cart Path Only” or “90-Degree Rule” notifications before entering their designated holes, eliminating the need for memorization. The management team can also view real-time vehicle locations, promptly identifying vehicles that linger too long, deviate from their paths, or enter restricted areas.

While this method doesn’t completely eliminate the impact of rain on the pace of play, it reduces accidental entry onto greens, repetitive explanations, and manual patrols, making rule enforcement easier.
